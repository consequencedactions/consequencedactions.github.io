I imagine free will the way I imagine fog thinking it invented the road.\
It moves forward, convinced the world is appearing because of it, unaware that the asphalt was always there, patient, waiting to be seen.

Inside me, decisions don’t arrive as moments, they seep like mold in old walls, you only notice when something turns black. I say I chose, but what I mean is the accumulation finally tipped. The body already knew. The nerves had voted long before the mouth announced the result.

Memory is not an archive, its a biased witness.\
It rearranges causes into a confession that often flatters me. I remember intention the way a dream remembers plot, retroactively, with confidence. Even regret feels taught, rehearsed, as if guilt were proof of authorship. As if pain could certify freedom.

Sometimes I think consciousness is just the echo of a decision made elsewhere. A cathedral built around a sound that already happened. I walk through it, impressed by the shapes on the ceiling, mistaking reverberation for voice.

Choice feels most real when it hurts.\
As if suffering were the signature of the self, but even fire follows rules. Even wounds obey physics. Pain doesn’t prove freedom, it demands attention. The universe hurts all the time without meaning to.

And still there are cracks.

Tiny, unelegant fissures where habit forgets about itself.\
Where I hesitate not because I’m unsure, but because something in me refuses to be fluent. A stutter in causality. A breath held too long. In those instants, I don’t feel free, I feel unprotected. Like standing between two mirrors that refuse to show my reflection.

Maybe free will isn’t a power but a delay.\
A fraction of a second where the chain loosens, not enough to escape, but enough to feel it pull. Enough to know I am attached. Freedom as awareness of the know, not its undoing.

I don’t choose the ocean, but sometimes i choose how long i stay under water.\
I don’t decide the tide, but I decide whether I learn its language or let it name me entirely.

So when I say I chose, what i really mean is this,\
there was a moment where I saw the machinery and still stepped forward.\
Not because I was uncaused, but because I was awake.

and maybe that’s all the free will we get,\
not authorship, not sovereignty, just lucidity.

A thin, burning light between impulse and excuse, where the self briefly exists, and cannot look away.
